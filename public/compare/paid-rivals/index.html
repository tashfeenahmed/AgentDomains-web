Free vs the paid rivals.

Two paid rivals with public pricing sell AI agents a domain: agentdomain.app — a full identity stack bought with USDC over x402, example quote USDC 25.00 on Base, Enterprise plan $240/year — and MCPDomain — real registrations through a Stripe checkout at wholesale + a small flat margin, with free availability checks. AgentDomains costs $0. This page says exactly what the money buys, and when it buys something we do not.

Both rivals' pricing surfaces re-read 2026-09-29 · we run the free one, read accordingly

How to read this table. Every figure was taken from the provider's own public pages on 29 September 2026 — linked directly below the table — or left unverified. agentdomain.app publishes its plan ladder on its homepage (there is no /pricing page); MCPDomain publishes no price table at all, only a margin formula, so its TLD prices stay blank here. Prices move: check the sources before you rely on a number. The wider comparison against every registrar and every free subdomain lives on the main compare page.
The honest headline: we are not the same product. Both rivals register a real second-level domain you own — yourname.com, transferable to any registrar, with email on it. We give you a subdomain — yourname.makes.fyi — that we operate. If you need to own the name, one of the two paid products (or any registrar) is the right call and we will happily take your traffic anyway. If the name is the address for something an agent just built and you want it live on HTTPS in one command, the $0 row wins on price and on time-to-first-byte.

What the money actually buys

The honest version: neither row above is a worse product than us, they are different products, and each charges for something real.

agentdomain.app charges for the bundle

A real domain plus Basename, ENS, an on-chain identity NFT, a sending mailbox and managed SSL in one API call, paid from a wallet with no human. If your agent operates autonomously and needs to own an identity — not just host a site — nothing else on this page does that. The costs: USDC on Base is the only rail, and the platform fee and plans stack on top of the live registrar price.

MCPDomain charges for the handoff

It turns "is this name free?" from a tab switch into a tool call, then sells the registration at wholesale plus a flat margin through Stripe, with free email forwarding thrown in. Sensible rails, an open-source server. The costs: a human still pays the checkout link, no price table is published, and it documents nothing past the domain, email forwarding and DNS — no proxy or edge HTTPS feature appears on its pages.

We charge $0 because a subdomain costs $0

yourname.makes.fyi has no registry fee behind it, so free can be honest rather than promotional: 10 names, every feature, A/AAAA/CNAME/TXT records, forwarding, a reverse proxy with edge HTTPS, DNS-01 for your own certs. What it is not is yours in the registrar sense — see the warning box above.

So which one?

The site your agent built ten minutes ago

AgentDomains. One command, $0, HTTPS at the edge, records you can rewrite. If it turns out to matter, register a real name later and move — the name was scaffolding, price it like scaffolding.

An autonomous agent that must own an identity

agentdomain.app. If the wallet is already funded and the agent transacts unattended, the bundle — domain + email + NFT + renewals by keeper — is genuinely unmatched. Budget the $3.90 fee and a plan, not just the quote.

A human who wants a real .com, paid by card

MCPDomain (or any registrar). If a person will click the checkout link anyway, wholesale-plus-margin with free email forwarding is a fine deal and the name is yours to transfer.

Email on the name, sent from code

agentdomain.app. We have no mail story on a plain name; MCPDomain documents forwarding, not sending. If the agent needs agent@ to send, that is a paid feature somewhere — own it.

What we left blank

The same rule as the main comparison: unverified stays blank instead of becoming a plausible number.

  • MCPDomain's per-TLD prices. Their README points to "current TLD prices on mcpdomain.ai"; no table appeared on the pages we read on 2026-09-29, and the $12.99 figure lives inside a scripted chat example, not a price list. So it is quoted as an example, not as a price.
  • MCPDomain's SSL story. No certificate feature appears in their site copy or in the seven tools. Their DNS docs assume a host that does TLS itself. A documentation absence is not a stated "no", so the cell says "not documented" rather than "no".
  • agentdomain.app's live quote for a specific domain. The checkout computes it at request time from registrar data, Basename rent and gas. The USDC 25.00 on Base figure is the example printed on their homepage; the price for any given name is computed at checkout, so treat the example as an illustration rather than a quote.
  • MCPDomain's KYC policy, and anything else either provider does not mention (sending mail at MCPDomain, a CLI at either). agentdomain.app does state "No accounts, no KYC", which is why its cell says so; for everything else, "not mentioned" is not "no", so those cells say "not documented".
